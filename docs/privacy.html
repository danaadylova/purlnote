Privacy policy

Effective 4 November 2026 · Earlier versions

In short: Purlnote has no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. Your patterns and notes stay on your device. If you connect a Ravelry account, the app talks directly to Ravelry on your behalf, and you can stop that at any time. I don't collect, sell or share your data.

Who makes Purlnote

Purlnote is made by Dana Adylova. You can reach me at unraveled.makes@gmail.com.

What stays on your device

These are stored only on your device, in the app's private storage, and never leave it, except for the optional Ravelry notes backup described below:

  • Pattern PDFs you import or download
  • Highlights, pen marks and notes on your patterns
  • Row and repeat counters, the row ruler's position, and where you are in each pattern
  • Step-by-step instructions, which are made on your device (by Apple's on-device model or by the app's own rules). Your patterns are never uploaded.
  • Projects, notes and photos you keep without Ravelry

Deleting the app deletes all of this data. Anything saved in your Ravelry account (see below) stays there until you remove it on Ravelry.

If you connect a Ravelry account

Connecting Ravelry is optional. If you do, the app reads and changes your Ravelry data only when you use it, and sends it directly to Ravelry:

  • Projects, stash, queue and purchased patterns are copied to your device so they work offline. Changes you make are sent back to Ravelry.
  • Photos you add to a Ravelry project are uploaded to that project on Ravelry.
  • Comments: when you choose to post a note as a project comment, it is published on Ravelry, where other people can see it.
  • Notes backup: while it's on, a copy of your notes, highlights, counters and reading positions is saved in your own Ravelry account, so you can get them back on a new device. Pen marks and PDFs are not backed up.

You can turn this off at any time under More:

  • Switch off notes backup to stop copying your notes to Ravelry.
  • Sign out of Ravelry to stop syncing altogether. The copies of your Ravelry projects, stash and queue are removed from your device; your PDFs, notes and counters stay.

What happens to data on Ravelry is covered by Ravelry's privacy policy. Purlnote is not made by or affiliated with Ravelry.

Signing in

Ravelry sign-in happens on Ravelry's own page, in your device's secure sign-in window, so Purlnote never sees your password. Your sign-in tokens are kept in your device's secure keychain.

The keychain can outlast the app: on iPhone and iPad, deleting an app doesn't remove what it saved there. Purlnote clears its old sign-in the next time it's installed, so a reinstalled app always starts signed out. To remove the sign-in straight away, sign out of Ravelry under More before deleting the app.

Ravelry requires a private app key to complete sign-in, and an app on your device can't keep a key private. So one step of sign-in goes through a small function I run on Cloudflare: it adds the key and passes the request to Ravelry. It does not store or log your sign-in details. Like any web service, Cloudflare handles the network connection, including your IP address, to deliver the request.

Photos and camera

Purlnote asks for access to your photos or camera only when you add a photo to a project, and only uses the photos you pick.

Feedback emails

If you email me, I'll see your email address and what you write, and use them only to reply and improve the app. Emails sent with More → Send feedback include the app version and system version, which you can remove before sending.

What Purlnote doesn't do

  • No Purlnote account, and no servers that store your data
  • No advertising, analytics or tracking, and no third-party code that does these
  • No selling or sharing of your data

Children

Purlnote is not directed at children under 13, and I don't knowingly collect information from them.
